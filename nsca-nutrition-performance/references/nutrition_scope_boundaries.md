# Nutrition Scope Boundaries

This skill provides general education and planning frameworks. It does not provide medical nutrition therapy.

## Refer To A Qualified Professional

Refer to a physician, registered dietitian, or appropriate clinician when:
- Eating disorder signs are present
- RED-S risk is present
- Rapid or extreme weight loss is requested
- The athlete is a minor seeking weight loss
- Medical disease affects diet
- Pregnancy is involved
- Severe or persistent GI symptoms occur
- Food allergy management is needed
- Supplement-drug interactions may exist
- The athlete asks whether a supplement is banned or legally permitted

## Safe Language

Use:
- "general range"
- "starting point"
- "education"
- "discuss with a registered dietitian"
- "check governing-body rules"

Avoid:
- "prescription"
- "treatment"
- "guaranteed safe"
- "banned-substance cleared"
- "diagnosis"

## Food-First Principle

Prioritize:
- Adequate total energy
- Carbohydrate matched to training demand
- Protein distributed across the day
- Healthy fat intake
- Fluids and electrolytes
- Micronutrient sufficiency through varied foods

Supplements should not compensate for poor foundational nutrition.

## Body Composition Boundary

Acceptable:
- Discuss performance-centered habits
- Use slow, conservative changes
- Monitor energy, recovery, mood, and performance
- Encourage qualified support

Avoid:
- Aggressive deficits
- Weight-class manipulation protocols
- Shame-based language
- Plans for athletes with eating disorder or RED-S risk
