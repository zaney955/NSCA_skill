# Plyometric Training

Use plyometrics to develop rapid force production through ballistic or stretch-shortening cycle actions.

## Prerequisites

Check:
- Training status
- Landing skill
- Strength and body mass context
- Injury restrictions
- Surface and footwear
- Available supervision

Novices should start with landing mechanics, low-intensity jumps, and low volume.

## Modes

Lower body:
- Jumps: two-foot takeoff and landing
- Hops: one-foot takeoff and same-foot landing
- Bounds: one-foot takeoff to opposite-foot landing
- Depth jumps/hops: gravity-assisted high-intensity options

Upper body:
- Medicine ball throws
- Slams
- Explosive push variations
- Explosive pull variations

Trunk:
- Medicine ball throws and short-range ballistic trunk actions when appropriate

## Intensity Factors

Intensity increases with:
- Higher landing forces
- Greater jump height or distance
- Single-leg contacts
- Higher entry velocity
- Direction changes
- Reactive/rebound actions
- Greater external load
- Higher complexity
- Depth jump height

## Volume

Track lower-body plyometric volume by foot contacts. Track throws by repetitions. Higher intensity requires lower volume and more rest.

## Frequency

General heuristic:
- Beginner: 1-2 sessions/week
- Intermediate: 2 sessions/week
- Advanced: 2-3 sessions/week if recovery and sport load allow

## Rest

Use enough rest to preserve quality:
- More rest for high-intensity jumps, bounds, and depth jumps
- Less rest for low-intensity extensive work

## Progression

Progress from:
- Stable landing to rebound
- Bilateral to unilateral
- Low amplitude to higher amplitude
- Simple direction to multidirectional
- Planned to reactive when appropriate
- Low volume to moderate volume

## Regression

Regress when:
- Landing alignment deteriorates
- Ground contact becomes slow for fast SSC goals
- Soreness or fatigue accumulates
- Athlete loses confidence
- Pain appears

## Integration

Place plyometrics:
- After warm-up
- Before heavy strength work when quality is the goal
- Away from heavy fatigue
- With strength or speed sessions when compatible
