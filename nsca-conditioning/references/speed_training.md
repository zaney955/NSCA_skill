# Speed Training

Speed development targets high movement velocity. Separate acceleration and maximal-velocity needs.

## Core Concepts

Acceleration:
- Increase velocity from stationary or slow movement
- Longer ground contacts than maximal velocity
- Horizontal force orientation is important

Maximal velocity:
- Upright sprint mechanics
- Short ground contacts
- High stiffness and rapid force application

## Training Priorities

Develop:
- Sprint mechanics
- Force production
- Rate of force development
- Horizontal and vertical impulse
- Relaxed high-speed coordination
- Recovery between high-quality reps

## Session Placement

Speed should be trained:
- Early in the session
- When the athlete is fresh
- After a specific warm-up
- With full or near-full recovery between reps

## Methods

Acceleration:
- Short sprints
- Starts from varied positions
- Resisted sprints when technically appropriate
- Hill sprints if surface and mechanics are safe

Maximal velocity:
- Flying sprints
- Build-up sprints
- Wicket or rhythm drills
- Sprint-float-sprint variations

Assisted sprinting:
- Use cautiously
- Avoid speeds that degrade mechanics

## Volume And Rest

High-quality sprinting requires low-to-moderate volume and adequate rest. Stop or reduce work when sprint time, rhythm, or mechanics degrade.

## Common Technical Priorities

Acceleration:
- Positive shin angles
- Strong projection
- Full extension
- Effective arm action
- Progressive rise

Max velocity:
- Tall posture
- Front-side mechanics
- Stiff ground contact
- Relaxed shoulders and hands
- Foot strike near the center of mass

## Monitoring

Use:
- Split times
- Best rep versus later reps
- Athlete RPE
- Technical quality
- Soreness and recovery
