# Aerobic Endurance Training

Aerobic endurance training improves the ability to produce energy oxidatively and sustain or recover from work.

## Program Variables

Define:
- Mode
- Frequency
- Intensity
- Duration
- Volume
- Progression
- Recovery

## Modes

Choose based on sport and constraints:
- Running
- Cycling
- Rowing
- Swimming
- Circuit-based conditioning
- Tempo intervals
- Intermittent field conditioning

## Intensity Approaches

Use:
- Heart-rate zones
- RPE
- Maximal aerobic speed
- Lactate or ventilatory threshold data when available
- Pace or power

## Training Types

Long slow distance:
- Builds general aerobic capacity
- Can be useful early in preparation or for endurance athletes

Pace/tempo:
- Sustained work at controlled intensity
- Useful for economy and threshold-related goals

Interval:
- Alternates work and rest
- Can target aerobic power, threshold, or repeated high-intensity work

Fartlek:
- Variable intensity
- Useful when structure and sport variability both matter

## Season Application

Off-season:
- Build general capacity and tolerance

Preseason:
- Shift toward sport-specific conditioning

In-season:
- Maintain capacity with lower volume and careful fatigue management

Postseason:
- Restore and transition

## Interference Considerations

High volumes of endurance work may interfere with strength and power development. Manage:
- Total volume
- Session order
- Recovery time
- Mode selection
- Block emphasis

## Monitoring

Use:
- RPE
- Heart rate response
- Pace
- Time to fatigue
- Recovery between intervals
- Session duration
