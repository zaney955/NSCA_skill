# Periodization Models

Periodization organizes training over time. Programming defines the specific modes, methods, loads, and sessions.

## Hierarchy

- Annual plan: full year view
- Macrocycle: several months to a year
- Mesocycle: usually 2-6 weeks
- Microcycle: usually several days to 2 weeks, commonly 1 week
- Training day: one day, may include multiple sessions
- Training session: the actual workout

## Periods And Seasons

Typical mapping:
- Off-season: build work capacity, hypertrophy, strength foundation
- Preseason: shift toward sport-specific strength, power, and conditioning
- In-season: maintain key qualities, minimize fatigue, support competition
- Postseason: active rest, restoration, address deficits

## Common Models

Parallel model:
- Multiple qualities trained together.
- Useful for novice/developmental athletes and broad preparation.

Sequential model:
- One or a few qualities emphasized in a sequence.
- Useful when advanced athletes need stronger stimulus and clearer phase goals.

Emphasis model:
- Multiple qualities are trained, but emphasis rotates.
- Useful for intermediate/advanced team sport athletes with congested schedules.

## Phase Emphasis

Common resistance sequence:
1. Hypertrophy or strength-endurance
2. Basic strength
3. Strength-power
4. Peaking or maintenance
5. Active rest

Not every athlete needs every phase. Match the plan to sport, training status, and calendar.

## Microcycle Design

Include:
- Primary training days
- Lighter technical or recovery sessions
- Sport practice and competition
- Recovery days
- Testing or monitoring

Avoid placing high-volume lower-body strength immediately before key speed sessions or competitions unless the goal is deliberate overload.

## Annual Plan Workflow

1. Mark key competitions.
2. Mark season phases.
3. Divide into macrocycles.
4. Create mesocycles with primary goals.
5. Create representative microcycles.
6. Assign tests and monitoring points.
7. Add deloads and transition periods.

## Peaking And Tapering

Peak performance is time-limited. Reduce fatigue while preserving fitness:
- Lower volume
- Maintain some intensity
- Preserve speed and technical sharpness
- Avoid novel exercises near competition

## Adjustment Rules

Move from planned to responsive programming when:
- Competition schedule changes
- Athlete readiness shifts
- Injury restrictions emerge
- Testing shows a different limiter than expected
