# Resistance Training Variables

Use these variables to construct sessions and training blocks.

## Step 1: Needs Analysis

Define sport demand, athlete status, constraints, and primary goal before prescribing exercises or loads.

## Step 2: Exercise Selection

Categories:
- Core exercises: large muscle mass, multijoint, high transfer potential
- Assistance exercises: smaller muscle groups, weak links, hypertrophy, balance
- Structural exercises: load the spine directly or indirectly
- Power exercises: explosive, high skill, usually early in session

Selection criteria:
- Goal and sport demand
- Movement pattern
- Athlete technique skill
- Equipment
- Time
- Injury restrictions
- Fatigue from sport practice

## Step 3: Training Frequency

General heuristic:
- Beginner: 2-3 resistance sessions/week
- Intermediate: 3-4 sessions/week
- Advanced: 4+ sessions/week when recovery and schedule allow
- In-season: often 1-3 sessions/week with lower volume

Adjust for:
- Training status
- Season
- Total sport load
- Exercise stress
- Recovery capacity
- Session duration

## Step 4: Exercise Order

Default order:
1. Power, speed-strength, or high-skill lifts
2. Core strength exercises
3. Assistance exercises
4. Trunk, prehab, or low-risk accessories

Alternative order may prioritize a weakness, rehab constraint, or technical teaching goal.

## Step 5: Load And Repetitions

General goal zones:
- Max strength: heavy loads, low reps
- Power: light to moderate or heavy depending on exercise, maximal intent, low fatigue
- Hypertrophy: broad load range, moderate to high volume
- Muscular endurance: lighter loads, higher reps
- Maintenance: lower volume, sufficient intensity

Use 1RM percentages only when a valid 1RM exists. Otherwise use RM, RPE/RIR, bar speed, or technical quality.

## Step 6: Volume

Volume depends on training goal, status, phase, and recovery. Manipulate:
- Sets
- Reps
- Exercises
- Sessions per week
- Volume-load

Higher volume is useful for hypertrophy and work capacity, but can interfere with speed, power, and in-season readiness.

## Step 7: Rest Periods

General heuristic:
- Max strength and power: longer rest, often 2-5 minutes
- Hypertrophy: moderate rest, often 30-90 seconds or more depending on load
- Muscular endurance: shorter rest, often <= 30-60 seconds
- Skill learning: enough rest to preserve technique

Prioritize movement quality for power, speed, Olympic-lift derivatives, and high-risk exercises.

## Session Quality Rules

Reduce load or volume when:
- Technique breaks down
- Bar speed drops substantially for power work
- Athlete reports unusual pain
- Soreness or fatigue affects warm-up quality
- Competition or hard practice is near

Stop and refer when:
- Sharp pain, neurological symptoms, dizziness, chest pain, or post-surgical contraindications appear.
