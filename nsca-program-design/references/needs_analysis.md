# Needs Analysis

A needs analysis has two parts: sport analysis and athlete analysis.

## Sport Analysis

Identify:
- Movement patterns and planes
- Primary muscle groups and joints
- Strength, power, hypertrophy, and muscular endurance priorities
- Speed, change-of-direction, agility, endurance, and mobility requirements
- Common injury sites and mechanisms
- Position or event-specific demands
- Competition schedule and season phase

## Movement Analysis

Describe the recurring sport actions:
- Squat/hinge/lunge/push/pull/carry/brace/rotate
- Bilateral or unilateral
- Vertical, horizontal, lateral, or rotational force orientation
- Acceleration, deceleration, landing, cutting, contact, throwing, striking
- Open-chain or closed-chain emphasis

## Physiological Analysis

Rank the importance of:
- Maximal strength
- Rapid force development
- Power
- Hypertrophy
- Muscular endurance
- Aerobic endurance
- Repeated sprint ability
- Anaerobic capacity

## Injury Analysis

Identify common risk areas:
- Overuse from repetitive workload
- Contact/collision demands
- Landing and deceleration demands
- Shoulder/elbow stress in throwing sports
- Tendon, stress fracture, or joint instability history

Do not diagnose or treat injuries. Use this analysis to avoid obvious training conflicts and to identify when medical input is needed.

## Athlete Analysis

Collect:
- Age and maturation status if youth
- Training age and technique competence
- Current program history
- Strength and performance tests
- Body mass changes if relevant
- Injury restrictions and contraindications
- Equipment access
- Available training days and session duration
- Practice and competition schedule

## Training Status Heuristic

Beginner:
- No current training or only recent exposure
- Minimal technique skill
- Low tolerance for high training stress

Intermediate:
- Several months of consistent training
- Basic technique
- Moderate tolerance and recovery capacity

Advanced:
- One or more years of consistent training
- High technique competence
- Higher stress tolerance but smaller adaptation window

## Goal Selection

Choose the primary goal by combining:
- Sport demands
- Season phase
- Test results
- Athlete weaknesses
- Recovery capacity
- Time available

When many goals matter, choose a primary emphasis and maintain secondary qualities.
