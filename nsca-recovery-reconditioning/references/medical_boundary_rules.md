# Medical Boundary Rules

## Immediate Referral Flags

Refer to qualified medical professionals for:
- Acute injury
- Sharp pain
- Chest pain
- Fainting, dizziness, or unexplained shortness of breath
- Neurological symptoms
- Swelling, instability, or loss of function
- Fever or systemic illness
- Suspected concussion
- Post-surgical status without clearance
- Persistent unexplained underperformance
- Signs of disordered eating or RED-S risk
- Severe mood changes or mental health concern

## Reconditioning Boundary

A strength and conditioning plan may be written only when:
- The athlete is medically cleared for the relevant activities, or
- A qualified clinician has supplied indications and contraindications.

If not, produce a communication checklist instead of a training plan.

## Language Rules

Use:
- "concern"
- "risk flag"
- "training implication"
- "refer"
- "medical clearance needed"

Avoid:
- "diagnosis"
- "treatment"
- "cleared to play"
- "safe to return"
- "injury is healed"

## Communication With Allied Health

Ask:
- What activities are allowed?
- What activities are contraindicated?
- Are there ROM, load, impact, or contact limits?
- What symptoms require stopping?
- What criteria must be met before progression?
- Who makes return-to-play decisions?

## Documentation

Record:
- Date
- Source of medical guidance
- Restrictions
- Program changes
- Athlete response
- Questions for medical staff
