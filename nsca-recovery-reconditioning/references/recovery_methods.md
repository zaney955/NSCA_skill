# Recovery Methods

Recovery strategy should match the cause of fatigue.

## Highest Priority

Sleep:
- Protect sleep opportunity
- Reduce late-day stress when possible
- Avoid adding hard training when sleep is poor for repeated nights

Nutrition and hydration:
- Ensure adequate energy availability
- Support glycogen restoration when training volume is high
- Support protein intake for training adaptation
- Address hydration basics

Training load management:
- Adjust volume, intensity, density, and exercise selection
- Add deloads or recovery days
- Remove unnecessary novelty

Psychological recovery:
- Reduce avoidable stressors
- Use clear communication
- Support autonomy and confidence

## Modalities

Possible adjuncts:
- Light aerobic recovery
- Mobility work
- Massage
- Compression
- Cold-water immersion in selected contexts
- Heat exposure in selected contexts
- Relaxation or breathing drills

Adjunct modalities should not compensate for poor sleep, underfueling, or excessive training load.

## Strategy Selection

If soreness dominates:
- Reduce eccentric volume
- Use low-intensity movement
- Avoid adding high-impact work

If performance is down but soreness is low:
- Check sleep, stress, illness, and testing conditions
- Reduce intensity or volume depending on session goal

If motivation and mood are low:
- Reduce monotony
- Communicate expectations
- Consider psychological stress and referral when persistent

If competition is near:
- Reduce volume
- Maintain some intensity and speed
- Avoid novel exercises

## Monitoring

Track:
- Sleep
- Soreness
- Fatigue
- Mood/stress
- Session RPE
- Simple performance marker

Use trends rather than isolated readings.
