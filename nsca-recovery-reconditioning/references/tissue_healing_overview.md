# Tissue Healing Overview

This is a high-level training reference, not a rehabilitation protocol.

## Healing Phases

Inflammatory response:
- Pain, swelling, redness, reduced function may appear
- Damaged tissue begins cleanup and early healing
- Training should not overstress healing tissue

Fibroblastic repair:
- New collagen and connective tissue develop
- Tissue is still weaker and less organized
- Loading must be controlled and medically guided

Maturation and remodeling:
- Tissue gradually strengthens and aligns with stress
- Progression may continue for months
- Pain reduction does not necessarily mean full tissue readiness

## Training Implications

Strength and conditioning professionals should:
- Follow medical indications and contraindications
- Avoid overstressing healing tissue
- Progress by criteria and tolerance
- Communicate with the allied health team
- Maintain unaffected qualities when allowed

## Scope Boundary

Do not:
- Diagnose tissue status
- Estimate healing timelines as clearance
- Prescribe treatment for specific injuries
- Progress against medical restrictions

Do:
- Ask for clearance status
- Ask for allowed and prohibited activities
- Provide a conservative reconditioning structure
- Track response and communicate changes
