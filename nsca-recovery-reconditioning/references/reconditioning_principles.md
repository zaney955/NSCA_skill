# Reconditioning Principles

Reconditioning prepares the athlete for unrestricted training and competition after medical clearance or under allied health direction.

## Required Inputs

Before planning, confirm:
- Diagnosis if supplied by medical staff
- Clearance status
- Indications
- Contraindications
- Current allowed activities
- Pain or symptom rules
- Load or ROM restrictions
- Contact/impact restrictions
- Medical team contact process

## Core Principles

- Healing tissues must not be overstressed.
- Progression should be criteria-based.
- The plan must be individualized.
- The allied health team must communicate.
- Training should move from general to sport-specific.

## Progression Categories

1. Restore general capacity within restrictions
2. Rebuild strength and local endurance
3. Reintroduce power and rate of force development
4. Reintroduce impact and plyometric demands if cleared
5. Reintroduce speed, COD, and sport-specific conditioning if cleared
6. Integrate practice demands

## Criteria To Progress

Examples:
- No increase in symptoms after session
- Movement quality maintained
- Strength asymmetry improving when measured
- Tolerates current volume
- Medical team approves next exposure

## Criteria To Regress

- Pain increases
- Swelling or symptoms return
- Movement quality worsens
- Fatigue changes mechanics
- Athlete cannot complete assigned work
- Medical team changes restrictions

## Program Design Notes

Maintain unaffected areas when allowed:
- Contralateral limbs
- Trunk
- Upper or lower body depending on injury
- Aerobic conditioning using allowed modes

Progress carefully:
- Load before speed for some contexts
- Technique before intensity
- Bilateral before unilateral when needed
- Stable before unstable
- Planned before reactive
- Low impact before high impact

These are heuristics, not universal medical rules.
