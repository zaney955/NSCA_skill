# Spotting Guidelines

## Purpose

The spotter's primary responsibility is athlete safety. Motivation and forced reps are secondary.

## Exercises That Commonly Need Spotting

Free weight exercises may need spotting when:
- The implement moves over the face or neck
- The bar is on the back
- The bar is front-racked on the shoulders
- The implement moves overhead
- The load is heavy enough that a failed rep creates risk

Examples:
- Bench press
- Dumbbell press
- Back squat
- Front squat
- Shoulder press

## Exercises Not To Spot

Do not spot power exercises such as:
- Power clean
- Snatch
- Clean pulls caught dynamically
- Push jerk in many contexts

Instead:
- Use appropriate platform space
- Clear the area
- Teach safe misses
- Use qualified supervision

## Spotter Requirements

Spotters should:
- Understand the exercise
- Be strong enough and tall enough when relevant
- Know where to place hands
- Communicate before the set
- Help only as much as needed
- Maintain stable body position

## Communication Before The Set

Confirm:
- Exercise
- Target reps
- Whether liftoff is requested
- Liftoff count
- When to assist
- How the athlete will signal help

## Liftoff

Use a clear count. Ensure the athlete has control before releasing the implement.

## Number Of Spotters

One spotter is preferred when sufficient. Add spotters when:
- Load exceeds one spotter's safe capacity
- Exercise demands side spotters
- Athlete is attempting near-maximal loads

Multiple spotters must coordinate to avoid uneven assistance.

## Over-The-Face Barbell Exercises

The spotter should usually:
- Stand at the head
- Use a stable stance
- Use an alternated grip on the bar when assisting
- Help guide the bar safely to the rack

## Dumbbell Pressing

Spot near the wrists or dumbbells rather than pushing at the elbows. Elbow-only spotting may fail to protect the athlete if the elbow collapses.

## Rack Safety

For squats and overhead work:
- Use a rack when appropriate
- Set safety bars at a useful height
- Clear plates and objects from the lifting area
- Keep nonlifting athletes away from the zone
