# Common Errors And Cues

Use short cues. Correct one major issue at a time.

## Cue Types

External cue:
- Directs attention to outcome or environment.
- Often useful for performance.

Internal cue:
- Directs attention to body part.
- Useful when precise positioning is needed.

Task cue:
- Names the action simply.

## Squat

Error: knees collapse inward
- Cues: "Push the floor apart." "Track knees over toes."
- Checks: stance width, foot pressure, hip strength, load too heavy

Error: heels rise
- Cues: "Keep the whole foot heavy." "Sit between your feet."
- Checks: ankle mobility, stance, depth, load

Error: trunk collapses
- Cues: "Brace before you move." "Chest and hips rise together."
- Checks: load, bracing, upper back, fatigue

## Hinge

Error: bar drifts forward
- Cues: "Keep the bar close." "Shave the legs with the bar."
- Checks: lat tension, start position, hip hinge pattern

Error: lumbar rounding
- Cues: "Lock in your brace." "Move from the hips."
- Checks: load, mobility, fatigue, setup

## Press

Error: wrists bent back
- Cues: "Stack knuckles over forearm." "Punch straight."
- Checks: grip, bar position, load

Error: uneven press
- Cues: "Press both sides together." "Drive the bar evenly."
- Checks: grip symmetry, shoulder position, fatigue

## Pull

Error: shrugging
- Cues: "Put shoulders in your back pockets." "Pull elbows, not shoulders."
- Checks: load, scapular control, range

Error: jerking
- Cues: "Own the start." "Pause, then pull."
- Checks: load too heavy, tempo, trunk stability

## Lunge / Single-Leg

Error: knee caves
- Cues: "Show the logo on your shorts forward." "Knee follows shoelaces."
- Checks: hip control, foot pressure, step length

Error: unstable torso
- Cues: "Stand tall through the crown." "Move like an elevator."
- Checks: load, balance, stance width

## Power

Error: early arm pull
- Cues: "Legs first." "Arms are ropes."
- Checks: start position, load, teaching progression

Error: slow or grinding rep
- Cues: "Move fast or stop." "Reset and make it crisp."
- Checks: load too heavy, fatigue, exercise placement

## Safety Cues

Use stop cues when needed:
- "Rack it."
- "Set it down."
- "Reset."
- "That is enough for today."
