# Mobility And Flexibility Methods

## Performance Preparation

A warm-up should prepare the athlete for the session's demands. Common structure:
1. General movement to raise temperature
2. Mobility for relevant joints
3. Dynamic movement
4. Activation or coordination drills
5. Skill-specific rehearsal

## Dynamic Stretching

Use before training or competition when movement preparation is needed. Keep movements controlled and specific to the session.

## Static Stretching

Use when the goal is flexibility development or restoration. It may be less appropriate immediately before maximal power or speed work if long-duration static holds reduce performance readiness.

## Ballistic Stretching

Use cautiously and only when appropriate for trained athletes and sport demands. Avoid uncontrolled bouncing.

## Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation

PNF methods can improve ROM but require proper instruction and partner skill when assisted.

## Mobility Programming

Choose mobility drills based on:
- Movement requirement
- Observed limitation
- Athlete control
- Session goal
- Time available

## Common Warm-Up Errors

- Too long and fatiguing
- Too generic for the session
- Too little build-up before high intensity
- Ignoring skill rehearsal
- Using aggressive stretching that irritates symptoms

## Safety Boundary

If ROM is limited by pain, swelling, neurological symptoms, or recent injury, do not force mobility work. Refer to a qualified medical professional.
