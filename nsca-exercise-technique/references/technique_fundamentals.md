# Technique Fundamentals

## Grip

Common grips:
- Pronated: palms down / overhand
- Supinated: palms up / underhand
- Neutral: palms facing each other
- Alternated: one pronated, one supinated
- Hook: thumb trapped under fingers, often for weightlifting derivatives

Prefer a closed grip unless a specific exercise variation has a clear reason otherwise.

## Grip Width

Common options:
- Narrow
- Shoulder-width
- Wide
- Clean grip
- Snatch grip

Grip should support balance, joint position, and the goal of the exercise.

## Stable Body Position

Standing exercises:
- Feet stable
- Heels and balls of feet grounded when appropriate
- Trunk braced
- Movement controlled

Seated or supine bench exercises often use five points of contact:
- Head
- Shoulders / upper back
- Buttocks
- Right foot
- Left foot

## Machine Setup

Set machine axes and pads so the working joint aligns with the machine axis when applicable. Adjust:
- Seat
- Back pad
- Chest pad
- Roller pad
- Handles
- Range limiter

## Range Of Motion

Use the effective ROM that matches:
- Goal
- Athlete control
- Joint tolerance
- Sport need
- Equipment constraints

Avoid forcing ROM that causes pain or compensation.

## Movement Speed

Most strength and hypertrophy exercises:
- Controlled eccentric
- Stable transition
- Intentional concentric

Power exercises:
- Fast intent
- Low fatigue
- Technical control

## Breathing And Bracing

Default:
- Exhale through the difficult part of the concentric phase
- Inhale during the easier or eccentric phase

For experienced athletes using heavy structural lifts:
- Brief bracing and transient breath hold may support spinal position
- Avoid prolonged breath holding
- Avoid this strategy for athletes with relevant medical contraindications unless cleared by qualified professionals

## Weight Belts

Belts may be appropriate for:
- Heavy structural exercises
- Near-maximal or maximal loads
- Experienced lifters

Belts are usually unnecessary for:
- Light loads
- Exercises not stressing the trunk or low back
- Beginners learning basic bracing

## Teaching Order

1. Explain purpose.
2. Demonstrate.
3. Set position.
4. Practice unloaded or light.
5. Cue one error at a time.
6. Progress only when movement quality is stable.
