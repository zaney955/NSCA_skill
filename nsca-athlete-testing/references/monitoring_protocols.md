# Monitoring Protocols

Monitoring uses repeat measures to guide training.

## Monitoring Goals

Common goals:
- Track progress
- Detect fatigue
- Support readiness decisions
- Evaluate training effectiveness
- Inform deloads or load changes
- Communicate with coaches and allied health staff

## Measure Selection

Good monitoring tests are:
- Quick
- Repeatable
- Low fatigue
- Relevant to the sport
- Easy to interpret
- Practical in the training environment

## Common Monitoring Inputs

Performance:
- Countermovement jump
- Isometric strength
- Sprint split
- Submaximal bar velocity
- Repeated technical quality

Subjective:
- Sleep quality
- Soreness
- Fatigue
- Stress
- Mood
- Readiness

Training load:
- Session RPE
- Duration
- Volume-load
- Practice load

## Frequency

Examples:
- Daily: brief wellness, soreness, readiness
- 1-3 times/week: jump or submaximal performance
- Every block: full test battery
- Pre/post season phase: broader assessment

## Decision Rules

Use preplanned thresholds. Examples:
- If readiness is low and performance marker drops, reduce volume.
- If soreness is high but performance is stable, keep intensity moderate and reduce accessories.
- If performance improves and RPE is low, progress load gradually.

Do not make major decisions from one noisy data point.

## Data Hygiene

Maintain:
- Same protocol
- Same equipment
- Same time relative to training
- Clear athlete IDs
- Notes on unusual conditions

## Communication

Report monitoring as:
- What changed
- Why it matters
- What action is recommended
- How confident the recommendation is
